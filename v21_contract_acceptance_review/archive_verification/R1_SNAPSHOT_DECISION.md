## Authority and feasibility

A requires both an audited cooperative writer domain and a guarded membership/content cut, complete classified recursive inventory, read pins, original mandatory witnesses, exact crosslinks and closed recorded identity coverage. S requires that same cut but admits a demonstrably incomplete/conflicting graph and retains supported narrower facts. I permits justified single-source or source-group observations without a common cut. U records an aborted stronger acquisition after a known assurance/content/membership violation while retaining only safely grounded lower facts. X preserves a grounded run with no supportable attempt fact; lack of run grounding raises a typed error. The priority U > A/S > I > X and explicit per-claim authority make these distinctions operationally testable. S is stable contradictory evidence, not certification; U is instability, not an automatic assertion that an attempt was fraudulent.

The strongest claim is a count of recorded anchored identities in one visible covered set. It does not establish provider execution or historical lifetime completeness. A corrupted registered raw journal can coexist with an intact independent accounting graph and A scoped cardinality. An unknown extra potential attempt cannot be silently classified as an innocuous copy: it prevents graph closure. A hash authenticates no recording origin. These are explicit requirements in AUTHORITY and WITNESS_REQUIREMENTS, including the version-bound exact schemas and raw-byte versus logical-canonical digest domains.

The existing run/publication guard L1 is separate from the outer shared-ledger boundary guard candidate L2 and non-reentrant Ledger.lock L3. Holding L1 then L2 without holding L3 across `_verify_completed` lets its unchanged read helpers acquire/release L3 without recursive self-deadlock. Current source proves neither complete enrollment nor bounded five-second observer acquisition; both are future requirements. The direct Ledger API, gateway registration, witness hooks and other shared-ledger users must be enrolled/drained before A/S. No present guard boolean or existing graph VERIFIED can stand in for this proof. `R1_WRITER_DOMAIN_AUDIT.md` identifies these gaps precisely.

No required change to owner acquisition, fencing, lease release or exclusive publication follows from this design. The observer must be non-owning and must not construct metadata-writing Session/Publication objects. The read-only completed-verification core does not claim an owner; a future grounded facade must have no other owner's fd. F01 primitives remain unchanged. Integration may wrap/enroll cooperative callers under a separate authorized scope; if it cannot safely achieve full participation, I/X remains a defined, useful outcome. I found no proof that the declared scope makes every valid implementation impossible.

## Adversarial assessment

All twenty requested scenarios are adjudicated in `R1_DOWNGRADE_REVIEW.csv`, with source rules, remaining evidence, source certification limits, retry and preservation outcomes. These are independent prospective decision cases, not execution of an unimplemented protocol. Preexisting absence/conflict returns S when a cut and surviving facts exist; creation/deletion/replacement inside B..E is U and invalidates that epoch. No automatic polling until the fault disappears may restore A. Unproven coverage or failure to acquire a cut yields I/X or a typed operational error; it is not evidence of corruption. Read-pin failure cannot justify a successful capture. A guard timeout is bounded to two acquisitions and five seconds per guard wait; neither wait calls a provider.

Cooperating creation after enumeration must block on the guards. Pinning existing files alone cannot block new directory entries. Known ABA is a domain violation even if final hashes match. Unobserved malicious privileged ABA is outside the qualified cooperative trust model; requiring hostile-host prevention would invent a new objective. An observer killed during capture has returned no valid sealed view, and OS handles/guards must release; later verification starts a fresh capture without replay or ownership changes. Concurrent finalization serializes on L1; unrelated runs sharing the ledger serialize on L2, with full global ledger validation before own-run projection.

Capture after an existing RESULT must use the same guarded current source verification. A verified legacy source may coexist with S accounting when the stricter reservation requirement fails, because source eligibility and new accounting completeness are separate axes. I/U/X cannot produce a current eligible v2.1 terminal. Suspect immutable RESULT/prepared bytes remain forensic even when current verification rejects them. These requirements close the prior snapshot gap without reopening R2.
